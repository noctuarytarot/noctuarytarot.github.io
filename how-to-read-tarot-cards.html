How to Read Tarot Cards

A beginner’s guide

The fastest way to get comfortable with tarot is to stop trying to memorize seventy-eight fixed definitions and start reading the picture in front of you. Every card in a well-illustrated deck (the Rider-Waite-Smith tradition and most decks that follow it) tells a small visual story — figures, objects, weather, posture — and that story is usually a faster route to meaning than any keyword list.

Start with one card at a time. Look at it for a few seconds before reading any written meaning: what’s the mood? Is something moving toward you or away? Then read the short interpretation and notice where it agrees with your first impression and where it doesn’t — both are useful. Upright cards generally point to that energy flowing freely; reversed cards suggest the same theme turned inward, delayed, or asking for more patience, not a flat "opposite" meaning.

Once single cards feel familiar, try simple three-card spreads — past, present, future, or situation, action, outcome — and read them as a short sentence rather than three separate facts. The skill that actually matters isn’t recall, it’s noticing connections: which card changes how you read the one next to it.
