The Major Arcana Explained

22 cards, 22 big themes

The twenty-two Major Arcana are the part of the tarot deck most people recognize even without knowing the rest — The Fool, The Magician, Death, The Tower, The Star. They form a loose sequence often called "the Fool’s Journey," running from 0 (The Fool, pure potential and a leap into the unknown) to 21 (The World, completion and integration), with every card in between marking a stage of growth: love and choice (The Lovers), letting structures collapse so something truer can be built (The Tower), rest and recovery after a long effort (The Star).

Because they represent broad, archetypal forces rather than everyday events, Major Arcana cards tend to carry more weight in a reading. A spread dominated by Major Arcana usually signals that bigger, less controllable currents are at play — a real turning point rather than a small daily decision. A spread with few or no Major cards more often points to ordinary, manageable choices.

You don’t need to memorize all twenty-two in order to use them well. It helps to notice the handful you keep drawing — the cards that show up for you again and again often say more about a recurring pattern in your life than any single meaning ever could.
